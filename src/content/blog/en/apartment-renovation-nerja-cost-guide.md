## What Permits Do You Need for a Renovation in Nerja?

Navigating Spanish bureaucracy can be challenging, especially for expats. For any renovation work in Nerja, you will need to obtain the appropriate permits from the local Town Hall (Ayuntamiento de Nerja). The type of permit depends on the scope of your project.

*   **Obra Menor (Minor Works Permit)**: This permit is required for non-structural work that does not alter the building's exterior or change the use of the property. Examples include painting, replacing flooring, updating bathrooms or kitchens without changing their layout, and minor electrical or plumbing work. The application typically requires a basic project description and sometimes a simple plan.
*   **Obra Mayor (Major Works Permit)**: This is necessary for structural changes, alterations to the building's facade, changes in the property's use, or significant layout modifications that affect load-bearing walls. An Obra Mayor requires a detailed technical project signed by a qualified architect or technical architect (aparejador/arquitecto técnico). This process is more complex and time-consuming.

It is crucial to apply for and secure the correct permits before any work begins. Failing to do so can result in fines, forced demolition, or difficulties when selling the property. A professional renovation company will guide you through the permit application process, often handling it on your behalf. You can find general information on building regulations and permits through official Spanish government channels, such as the Ministry of Transport, Mobility and Urban Agenda (Ministerio de Transportes, Movilidad y Agenda Urbana).

## The Renovation Process: Step by Step

Understanding the typical stages of an apartment renovation will help you manage expectations and track progress.

### Initial Consultation and Design

The process begins with a consultation to discuss your vision, requirements, and budget. Our design team will help you explore layout options, material choices, and aesthetic preferences. This stage often involves creating detailed plans and 3D renderings.

### Permitting and Planning

Once the design is finalised, the necessary permits are applied for. This can take several weeks or even months for an Obra Mayor. During this time, detailed schedules, material orders, and subcontractor arrangements are made.

### Demolition and Structural Work

With permits in hand, demolition begins. Old fixtures, flooring, and non-load-bearing walls are removed. If structural changes are part of the plan, these are executed at this stage, always under the supervision of qualified professionals.

### Installations (Electrical, Plumbing)

New electrical wiring, plumbing systems, and HVAC installations are fitted. This is a critical phase where the 'bones' of your new apartment are put in place, ensuring modern efficiency and safety.

### Finishes (Tiles, Flooring, Painting)

Once the core installations are complete, the finishing work begins. This includes plastering, installing new windows and doors, laying porcelain tiles, other flooring, and painting. This is where your design choices truly come to life.

### Final Touches and Handover

The final stage involves installing custom kitchens, complete bathroom renovations, light fittings, and any bespoke carpentry. A thorough clean-up is performed, and a final inspection ensures everything meets quality standards before the keys are handed over.
