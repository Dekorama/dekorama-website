## Understanding Permits and Regulations for Renovations in Mijas
Navigating the Spanish permit system is a critical step for any renovation. In Mijas, as with other municipalities, you will need a municipal license (licencia de obra) before starting work. There are two main types:

*   **Licencia de Obra Menor (Minor Works Permit)**: Required for smaller, non-structural renovations that do not alter the building's footprint, volume, or structural elements. Examples include changing tiles, painting, or updating bathrooms/kitchens without layout changes.
*   **Licencia de Obra Mayor (Major Works Permit)**: Necessary for significant structural changes, extensions, façade modifications, changes to the building's use, or projects requiring an architect's involvement. This permit involves more extensive documentation and a longer approval process.

Your chosen renovation company should guide you through the permit application process, preparing the necessary plans and submitting them to the Mijas Town Hall (Ayuntamiento de Mijas). It is illegal to commence major renovation work without the appropriate permit, and doing so can result in significant fines and orders to cease work or even revert changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an architect for a full renovation in Mijas?
For major structural changes, extensions, or significant modifications to a building's layout or façade, an architect's project and supervision are legally required in Spain. For minor cosmetic renovations, an architect may not be mandatory, but a reputable renovation company will advise you based on the scope of your project and local Mijas regulations.

### How long does a full renovation typically take?
The duration of a full renovation in Mijas varies significantly based on the project's complexity and size. A small apartment renovation might take 3-4 months, while a larger villa with extensive structural changes could take 6-8 months or more. This timeline includes permit acquisition, which can add several weeks to the initial phase.

### Can I live in my property during a renovation?
It is generally not advisable to live in your property during a full renovation, especially if it involves major structural work, plumbing, or electrical system overhauls. The disruption, dust, noise, and lack of essential services can make it uncomfortable and unsafe. Most clients arrange alternative accommodation for the duration of the project.

### What are the payment terms for a renovation company?
Standard payment terms usually involve an initial deposit (typically 10-30%) to secure the project and cover initial material costs. Subsequent payments are then linked to the completion of specific project milestones, such as demolition, structural work, or finishing stages. A final payment is made upon satisfactory completion of all work.

### Is it better to buy materials myself or let the company supply them?
While you might save a small percentage by sourcing materials yourself, it is generally more efficient and often more cost-effective in the long run to let your renovation company supply them. They have established supplier relationships, can secure trade discounts, and are responsible for material quality, delivery, and any defects. This also simplifies warranty claims.
