### Typical Project Phases and Durations

Understanding the different stages of a renovation helps in grasping the overall timeline:

*   **Planning and Design (1-2 months):** This phase involves initial consultations, architectural drawings, material selection, and detailed budgeting.
*   **Permit Acquisition (1-3 months):** Obtaining the necessary building permits from the Mijas Town Hall can be a lengthy process and often dictates the project start date.

## Navigating Permits and Regulations for Mijas Renovations

Navigating local permits and regulations is a critical step for any renovation in Mijas. In Spain, renovations are categorised into *obra menor* (minor works) and *obra mayor* (major works), each requiring different permits from the local *Ayuntamiento* (Town Hall).

*   **Obra Menor:** Typically for non-structural changes like painting, replacing flooring, or minor bathroom updates. These permits are simpler and quicker to obtain.
*   **Obra Mayor:** Required for structural changes, extensions, significant layout alterations, or any work affecting the building's exterior. This usually necessitates an architect's project and a more extensive application process.

It is essential to work with a renovation company that understands Mijas's specific urban planning regulations. They can guide you through the application process, prepare the necessary technical documentation, and ensure your project complies with all local laws. You can find more information on local regulations on the official Mijas Town Hall website: [https://www.mijas.es/portal/](https://www.mijas.es/portal/es/)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need an architect for a renovation in Mijas?
Yes, an architect is typically required for *obra mayor* (major works) renovations in Mijas, which involve structural changes, extensions, or significant layout alterations. For *obra menor* (minor works), an architect may not be mandatory, but their expertise can still be valuable for design and planning.

### What is the difference between *obra mayor* and *obra menor* permits?
*Obra mayor* permits are for major structural changes, extensions, or significant layout alterations, requiring an architect's project. *Obra menor* permits are for minor, non-structural cosmetic changes like painting, tiling, or replacing fixtures. The application process and requirements differ significantly.

### Can I live in my property during a full renovation?
Living in your property during a full renovation is generally not advisable, especially if structural work, plumbing, or electrical systems are being overhauled. It can be disruptive, unsafe, and uncomfortable. Planning for temporary accommodation is often a practical necessity.
