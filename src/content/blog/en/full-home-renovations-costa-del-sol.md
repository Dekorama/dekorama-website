## What Does a Full Home Renovation (Reforma Integral) Entail?

A 'reforma integral' in Spain means a complete overhaul of a property, going far beyond cosmetic changes. It involves transforming the entire living space, often including structural modifications, new layouts, and updated systems. This type of renovation is ideal for older properties that require significant modernisation or for owners who wish to completely redesign their home's functionality and aesthetics.

Key aspects typically covered in a full home renovation include:

*   **Demolition and structural work**: Removing walls, creating new openings, or reinforcing existing structures.
*   **New installations**: Updating electrical wiring, plumbing, and air conditioning systems to modern standards.
*   **Flooring**: Replacing old floors with new porcelain tiles in Málaga, wood, or other materials.
*   **Wall finishes**: Plastering, painting, and applying decorative finishes.
*   **Kitchen and bathroom remodelling**: Installing new custom kitchens and complete bathroom renovations, including fixtures, fittings, and tiling.
*   **Joinery**: Replacing doors, windows, and built-in wardrobes.
*   **Exterior work**: Depending on the property, this might involve facade renovation, terrace improvements, or landscaping.

This comprehensive approach ensures that every element of your home is brought up to date, enhancing both its value and your quality of life.

## How Long Does a Full Home Renovation Take in Spain?

The timeline for a full home renovation on the Costa del Sol is a common concern for property owners, especially expats managing projects from abroad. While every project is unique, a typical full renovation can take anywhere from 3 to 6 months. More complex or larger properties might require 8 months or even longer.

### Key Stages and Timelines

1.  **Planning and Design (4-8 weeks)**: This initial phase involves consultations, design proposals, material selection, and finalising plans. It is crucial for setting the project's direction.
2.  **Permit Application (4-12 weeks)**: Obtaining the necessary municipal permits from your local town hall can be a lengthy process. This timeline is largely dependent on the efficiency of the local administration.
3.  **Demolition and Structural Work (2-4 weeks)**: Once permits are secured, old structures are removed, and any new structural elements are put in place.
4.  **Installations (4-8 weeks)**: New electrical, plumbing, and HVAC systems are installed. This stage often includes rough-ins for kitchens and bathrooms.
5.  **Finishes (6-12 weeks)**: This is where your home truly takes shape. It involves plastering, flooring installation (e.g., porcelain tiles), painting, fitting new doors and windows, and installing custom kitchens and complete bathroom renovations.
6.  **Final Touches and Inspections (1-2 weeks)**: Cleaning, final inspections, and addressing any minor adjustments. This leads to the handover of your beautifully renovated home.

Effective project management is vital to keeping the renovation on schedule. An experienced company will coordinate all trades and materials, minimising delays and ensuring a smooth workflow from start to finish.

## Navigating Permits and Regulations in Spain

Understanding the legal requirements for renovations in Spain is crucial, particularly for foreign property owners. For a full home renovation, you will almost certainly need a 'Licencia de Obra Mayor' (Major Works Permit) from your local town hall (ayuntamiento). This permit is required for any work that affects the structure, layout, or exterior of the building.

### Key Steps for Obtaining Permits:

1.  **Project Submission**: Your chosen renovation company or architect will prepare and submit detailed plans and technical documentation to the municipal planning department.
2.  **Fees Payment**: Associated fees, calculated based on the project's budget and local regulations, must be paid.
3.  **Review and Approval**: The town hall will review the plans to ensure they comply with local urban planning regulations and building codes. This process can take several weeks or even months.
4.  **Licence Issuance**: Once approved, the 'Licencia de Obra Mayor' is issued, allowing work to commence legally.

Failing to obtain the correct permits can lead to significant fines, forced demolition, or difficulties when selling the property in the future. It is highly advisable to work with a local renovation company that has a deep understanding of Spanish building regulations and can manage the permit application process on your behalf. For general information on Spanish urban planning regulations, you can consult resources from the Junta de Andalucía's planning department or your local town hall's website.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an architect for a full renovation in Spain?
Yes, for a 'Licencia de Obra Mayor' (Major Works Permit), which is required for full renovations involving structural changes or layout modifications, an architect or technical architect must sign off on the project plans. This ensures compliance with building codes and safety regulations.

### Can I live in my property during a full renovation?
It is generally not advisable to live in your property during a full renovation. The work often involves significant dust, noise, and disruption, including periods without water, electricity, or functional bathrooms/kitchens. It is safer and more comfortable to arrange alternative accommodation.

### How do I ensure quality control during the renovation?
Choose a reputable company with a strong track record and clear communication channels. Regular site visits, if possible, and consistent updates from your project manager are crucial. A good company will have internal quality checks and address any concerns promptly to ensure high standards.

### What is the difference between 'obra menor' and 'obra mayor' permits?
'Obra menor' (Minor Works Permit) is for small-scale, non-structural changes like painting or minor repairs. 'Obra mayor' (Major Works Permit) is for significant work affecting the structure, layout, or exterior, such as a full home renovation.
