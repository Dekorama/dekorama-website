## Key Factors Influencing Renovation Costs
Several elements contribute to the final price tag of your [full home renovations on the Costa del Sol](/en/reformas-integrales). Understanding these can help you manage your budget effectively.

## Navigating Permits and Regulations in Torremolinos
Undertaking a renovation in Torremolinos requires adherence to local building regulations and the acquisition of necessary permits. This is a critical step, particularly for expats who may be unfamiliar with Spanish bureaucracy.

In Spain, renovations typically require a municipal licence, known as a *Licencia de Obra*. There are generally two types:

*   **Licencia de Obra Menor (Minor Works Permit)**: For non-structural changes like painting, tiling, changing bathroom fixtures, or minor kitchen updates. This is usually quicker and less expensive to obtain.

*   **Licencia de Obra Mayor (Major Works Permit)**: Required for structural changes, extensions, significant layout modifications, or any work affecting the building's stability or exterior. This permit involves more detailed plans from an architect or technical surveyor and a longer approval process.

It is crucial to apply for the correct permit before starting any work. Your renovation company, such as Dekorama, can guide you through this process, often handling the paperwork on your behalf. You can find general information on building regulations and permits through your local Town Hall (Ayuntamiento de Torremolinos) or by consulting the national Código Técnico de la Edificación (CTE) for broader building standards.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need an architect for a renovation in Torremolinos?**
An architect is usually required for major structural changes, extensions, or significant layout modifications that affect the building's stability or exterior. For minor cosmetic renovations, a technical surveyor or a qualified builder may suffice. Your renovation company can advise on specific project requirements.

**How can I save money on a full home renovation?**
You can save money by prioritising essential updates, choosing mid-range materials instead of luxury ones, and doing some non-specialised tasks yourself if you are capable. Reusing existing elements where possible and getting multiple quotes also helps. A clear plan from the start minimises costly changes.

**What is the typical payment schedule for a renovation project?**
Payment schedules usually involve an initial deposit (10-30%), followed by staged payments tied to project milestones (e.g., demolition complete, plumbing installed, finishes applied). A final payment is made upon satisfactory completion of all work. Always ensure the payment schedule is clearly outlined in your contract.

**Are there any grants or subsidies for renovations in Spain?**
Yes, Spain occasionally offers regional or national grants for renovations, particularly those focused on energy efficiency, accessibility, or historical preservation. These can vary year by year and by region. It is worth checking with the local Town Hall or a specialised agency for current programmes that might apply to your property.
