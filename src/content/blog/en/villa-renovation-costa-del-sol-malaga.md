## What Factors Influence the Cost of Your Villa Renovation?
Understanding the elements that drive renovation costs helps in effective budgeting. Here are the primary factors:

### Scope of Work
Major structural changes, extensions, or significant layout reconfigurations will naturally increase costs. Moving load-bearing walls, adding new floors, or altering the roofline requires extensive engineering work and more complex permits.

### Material Quality
The choice of materials has a substantial impact. Opting for high-quality [porcelain tiles in Málaga](/en/porcelanicos), bespoke cabinetry, designer fixtures, and advanced insulation will elevate the final cost but also enhance durability, aesthetics, and property value. Dekorama offers a wide range of [premium materials](/en/materiales-premium) to suit various budgets and styles.

### Kitchens and Bathrooms
These areas are typically the most expensive per square metre due to the concentration of plumbing, electrical work, tiling, and specialised fixtures. Investing in modern, efficient, and stylish [custom kitchens](/en/cocinas-a-medida) and [complete bathroom renovations](/en/banos-completos) significantly impacts both cost and property appeal.

### Installations and Systems
Upgrading electrical systems, plumbing, heating, ventilation, air conditioning (HVAC), and potentially adding solar panels or home automation systems contributes significantly to the budget. Older villas often require complete rewiring and replumbing to meet current Spanish regulations and modern living standards.

### Site Accessibility and Condition
If your villa is difficult to access, or if it requires extensive demolition and waste removal, labour and logistics costs can increase. The existing condition of the property, including any hidden structural issues, damp, or pest problems, can also lead to unforeseen expenses.

## How Long Does a Full Villa Renovation Take in Malaga?
The timeline for a full villa renovation on the Costa del Sol is as variable as the cost, typically ranging from 6 to 18 months. This duration includes everything from initial design to final inspections. Here is a general breakdown of the stages:

### Planning and Design (1-3 months)
This phase involves initial consultations, architectural drawings, interior design concepts, and material selection. This is a crucial stage for defining your vision and ensuring all details are captured.

### Permit Acquisition (2-6 months)
Obtaining the necessary *Licencia de Obra Mayor* (Major Works Permit) from the local *Ayuntamiento* (city council) can be a lengthy process. The timeline depends on the complexity of your project and the specific municipality (e.g., Benalmádena, Marbella, Fuengirola).

### Demolition and Structural Work (1-3 months)
Once permits are secured, demolition begins, followed by any necessary structural modifications, extensions, or major layout changes.

### New Installations (1-2 months)
This stage involves installing new electrical wiring, plumbing, HVAC systems, and any other essential infrastructure.

### Finishes (2-4 months)
This is where the villa starts to take shape. It includes plastering, flooring installation (such as [porcelain tiles in Málaga](/en/porcelanicos)), painting, and fitting of [custom kitchens](/en/cocinas-a-medida) and [complete bathroom renovations](/en/banos-completos).

### Final Touches and Inspections (1 month)
Installation of lighting, fixtures, final painting, landscaping, and thorough cleaning. The project concludes with final inspections and handover.

## Navigating Permits and Regulations for Renovations in Spain
Undertaking a full villa renovation in Spain requires adherence to local urban planning laws and building regulations. The most critical permit is the *Licencia de Obra Mayor*, or Major Works Permit. This is mandatory for any renovation involving structural changes, alterations to the facade, changes in use, or significant extensions.

The process typically involves an architect preparing a detailed project plan, which is then submitted to your local *Ayuntamiento* (e.g., Benalmádena, Marbella, Fuengirola). The council reviews the plans to ensure compliance with local zoning laws, building codes, and safety standards. This can be a complex and time-consuming process, often requiring multiple revisions.
